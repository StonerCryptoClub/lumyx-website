The teardown

Five places a page leaks money

The structural failures we find in almost every audit, not a client case study. Pick a marker.

01 — Above the fold

The page doesn't repeat the promise

Someone clicks an ad or a search result expecting one specific thing, and the first screen says something more general. The visitor doesn't reconcile the gap. They leave, and you pay for the click anyway.

Why it's expensive: you paid for the click before the mismatch happened.

02 — Load

The hero is the slowest thing on the page

Sitewide speed scores look acceptable while the one image, video embed, or web font that visitors actually wait on drags the first meaningful paint out. The average hides the page that matters.

Google's threshold: Largest Contentful Paint under 2.5 seconds.

03 — Friction

The form asks for more than the offer earns

A phone number required before any value has been demonstrated. Fields nobody reads the answers to. Every field past what the offer justifies is another moment for the visitor to decide it isn't worth it.

The test: could you follow up successfully without that field?

04 — Mobile

The phone gets the desktop layout, squeezed

Most paid traffic and most ecommerce checkouts happen on a phone, but the mobile view gets treated as a compressed afterthought: tap targets too close, forms that fight the keyboard, buttons buried below three scrolls of preamble.

Where it shows up: mobile converts far below desktop and nobody investigates why.

05 — Signal

Nothing is instrumented

No verified conversion events, so there's no way to tell which of the four problems above is actually costing the most. Every decision after this one is a guess dressed up as a redesign.

The consequence: you can't fix in priority order what you never measured.

Before you book

Straight answers

Why is there no portfolio on this page?

A gallery of screenshots proves almost nothing: you can't tell from an image whether a page loads fast, converts, or works on a phone. So this page measures itself live in your browser, against the same standards every build ships to. It's the one demo we can't fake.

Do you redesign existing sites, or only build new ones?

Both. A revamp keeps what's working in the business and rebuilds the site around how it actually converts. A bespoke build is for businesses with no site yet, or one too far behind to save. The audit tells us which one you need.

What platforms do you build ecommerce product pages on?

Shopify and custom or headless storefronts. The redesign focuses on the product page itself, the highest-leverage page in ecommerce, rather than a full-store rebuild unless the audit shows you need one.

How is this different from a normal web design agency?

Most agencies hand off a site and leave. We're the ones running paid traffic into it afterward, so we stay accountable for what the page does with that traffic, not just how it looked on launch day.

Do I have to run ads with you to get a site built?

No. The build is scoped and quoted as its own project and stands on its own. Many clients move into an ongoing optimization retainer afterward, but nothing about the build requires it.

What does it cost?

Scope-based: a fixed project fee, quoted after the audit once we know exactly what needs building. No generic package priced before we've seen the site.

What happens in the Free Growth Audit?

We run your current site, or your plan for a new one, against message match, load speed, form friction, mobile layout, and tracking coverage, then hand you a prioritized fix list. It's useful whether or not you build with us.
